Here's the shape I think dev-tool marketing is collapsing into: a barbell. Two heavy ends, and a middle that's quietly disappearing. If you're still funding the middle, you're spending against the grain of where attention and trust are actually going.

## The two ends that survive

One end is bot armies. Agents talking to agents, deciding in the middle of a task, with no human in the loop, which tool to import. It's a real audience, and it reads none of your marketing. It reads your docs, your types, your error messages, your MCP server. Winning there is technical and it scales: be legible, be actionable, be the thing the model reaches for first.

The other end is the warm handshake. A specific person, a direct pitch, a relationship earned over time. The founder who DMs a head of platform. The small off-record dinner. The community where the right forty people actually trust each other. This end doesn't scale, and that's the point. Its value comes from the fact that it can't be faked or automated.

## The middle is being hollowed out

Everything between those two ends is losing its footing, and it's losing it from both sides at once.

From the machine side: the agent doesn't care about your clever landing page, your nurture sequence, your gated ebook, or your mid-funnel webinar. None of it enters the decision. The entire apparatus of broad, human-targeted demand-gen is invisible to the fastest-growing decision-maker in your funnel.

From the human side: people are drowning in generated content, and they've recalibrated. The generic blog post, the templated outreach, the "thought leadership" that reads like it came out of the same model everyone else uses. All of it now signals *low* effort and gets discounted on sight. What a person still trusts is the specific and the earned: a real recommendation, a demonstrably useful tool, a conversation with someone who obviously knows the domain.

So the middle takes it from both directions, too human to matter to the machines and too generic to be trusted by the humans. That looks structural to me rather than cyclical.

## What to do about it

Pick your ends, and fund them properly.

On the machine end, treat agent-readiness as a channel with a budget: `llms.txt`, an MCP server, docs an agent can execute against, and measurement of whether agents actually select and use you. This is distribution now, not a science project.

On the human end, go further toward the personal than feels efficient. Founder-led outreach. Tiny, high-trust rooms. Community where you show up as a peer rather than a logo. Spend the time that doesn't scale, because not scaling is exactly what the humans are responding to.

Then starve the middle. The undifferentiated content mill, the spray-and-pray campaigns, the mid-funnel machinery built for a 2019 buyer's journey. Move that budget to the ends.

If I had to name the two things worth building right now, they'd be a way to get chosen by the agents and a small room of people who trust you. Everything in between looks more optional every month.
