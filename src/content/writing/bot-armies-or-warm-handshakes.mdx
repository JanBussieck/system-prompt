Here's the shape I think dev-tool marketing is collapsing into: a barbell. Two heavy ends, and a middle that's quietly disappearing. If you're still funding the middle, you're spending against the grain of where attention and trust are actually going.

## The two ends that survive

**One end is bot armies.** Agents talking to agents, deciding — in the middle of a task, without a human in the loop — which tool to import. This is a real audience you can market to, but it reads none of your marketing. It reads your docs, your types, your error messages, your MCP server. Winning here is technical and scalable: be legible, be actionable, be the default the model reaches for. It's marketing to machines, and it's as measurable as the machines are patient.

**The other end is the warm handshake.** A specific person, a direct pitch, a relationship earned over time. The founder who DMs a head of platform. The small off-record dinner. The community where the right forty people actually trust each other. This end is the opposite of scalable, and that's the point — its whole value is that it can't be faked or automated. High touch, high affinity, high trust.

## The middle is being hollowed out

Everything between those two ends is losing its footing, and it's losing it from both sides at once.

From the machine side: the agent doesn't care about your clever landing page, your nurture sequence, your gated ebook, or your mid-funnel webinar. None of it enters its decision. The entire apparatus of broad, human-targeted demand-gen is invisible to the fastest-growing decision-maker in your funnel.

From the human side: people are drowning in generated content, and they've recalibrated. The generic blog post, the templated outreach, the "thought leadership" that reads like it was produced by the same model everyone else uses — it now signals *low* effort and gets discounted on sight. The things a person still trusts are the specific and the earned: a real recommendation, a demonstrably useful tool, a conversation with someone who obviously knows the domain.

So the middle gets it from both directions. Too human to matter to the machines. Too generic to be trusted by the humans. That's not a dip; it's a structural hollowing.

## What to do about it

Pick your ends, and fund them properly.

On the machine end, treat agent-readiness as a real channel: `llms.txt`, an MCP server, docs an agent can execute against, and measurement of whether agents actually select and use you. This is distribution now, not a science project.

On the human end, go further toward the personal than feels efficient. Founder-led outreach. Tiny, high-trust rooms. Genuine community where you show up as a peer, not a logo. Spend the time that doesn't scale, because the un-scalable-ness is exactly what the humans are paying attention to.

And the middle — the undifferentiated content mill, the spray-and-pray campaigns, the mid-funnel machinery built for a 2019 buyer's journey — starve it. Move that budget to the ends.

The tell that this is real: the two things worth building right now are a way to be chosen by the agents, and a small room of people who trust you. Everything in between is optional, and getting more optional by the month.
