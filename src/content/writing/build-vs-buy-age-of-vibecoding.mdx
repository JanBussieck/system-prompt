For twenty years the build-vs-buy conversation had a comfortable shape. Building was expensive up front, so buying won on speed. That logic is now backwards, and if your positioning still assumes the old shape, you are losing deals you should be winning.

## What actually changed

Vibecoding didn't make building cheaper across the board. It made *starting* cheap and left *owning* exactly as expensive as it always was. The upfront cost that used to protect your "buy" pitch has mostly evaporated. A competent engineer with a good model can stand up something that looks like your product's core feature before lunch.

So the prospect's internal build advocate now has a working prototype and a compelling story: "We already have it, and it cost us a day." Your job is no longer to argue that building is slow. It is to make the total cost of ownership visible before the prototype ships to production and starts quietly accruing debt.

## The new checklist buyers should be using

When a buyer weighs build against buy today, three questions decide it, and none of them is "how long to a demo":

- **Time to trust, not time to demo.** How long until this thing is safe to put in front of a paying customer? Generation gets you to demo in hours and to trust in months. That gap is your product.
- **Maintenance surface.** Every generated feature is a feature the team now maintains, patches, and debugs at 2am. What is the ongoing tax, and who pays it when the person who prompted it has moved teams?
- **Agent-compatibility.** Increasingly the build advocate is not a person but an agent scaffolding a solution. Does the buyer's own tooling reach for your primitive, or reinvent it badly? A tool that agents already know how to wire up wins by default.

## Selling into a world where "build" is free to start

You cannot win by pretending building is hard anymore. You win by reframing the comparison from *creation* to *ownership*. Make the buyer feel the second Tuesday, not the first afternoon.

Concretely: publish the real maintenance stories. Show the CVE you patched that every DIY implementation is still exposed to. Quantify the edge cases baked into your defaults. Give the buyer's champion the ammunition to say, out loud, "yes we could build it, and here is the five-figure annual cost of keeping it alive that the prototype is hiding from us."

Building will keep getting cheaper to start. Owning will not. Sell ownership.
