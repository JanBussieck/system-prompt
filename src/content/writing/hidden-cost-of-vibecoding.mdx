Describe what you want, watch it appear. For a demo, vibecoding is unbeatable. For a system someone has to keep running at 2am on a Tuesday, the invoice comes later, and it rarely lands on the line item you expected.

Dev-tool marketers need to understand that bill in detail, because it's the strongest argument left. When a prompt generates a working surface in thirty seconds, "we save you time" stops being a differentiator. Everyone saves time now. The question is what happens after the time is saved.

## The second 80%

Generation is fantastic at the first 80% of a feature and nearly useless at the second. It gives you the happy path. The form submits, the chart renders, the endpoint returns a 200 when you feed it exactly what it expects. What it doesn't give you is the retry logic, the malformed input, the rate limit, the timezone bug, the browser that renders your canvas one pixel off, or the customer whose data breaks an assumption nobody wrote down.

That second 80% is where real engineering lives, and it's the part a generated demo hides. A tool that has already solved it across thousands of production deployments is selling every edge case its authors hit so that you don't have to.

## Nobody owns the generated code

Generated code has no owner. When it breaks there's no changelog, no upgrade path, and no one who understands why it was written that way, because it was never written that way on purpose. You inherit a codebase with the institutional memory of a goldfish.

A maintained dependency spreads that cost across every user. When the platform ships a breaking change, one team absorbs it and everyone else gets a version bump. Vibecode the same functionality and you volunteer to be that team, forever, for a problem that was never your core business.

## Verification takes years

The last hidden cost is the one that matters most in regulated or high-stakes work. You can generate a payments flow in an afternoon. You cannot generate the years of audits, the compliance posture, or the reputation that gets you waved through a buyer's security review. When the thing being built touches money, identity, or safety, who stands behind it is most of the purchase.

## What this means for how you sell

Speed is table stakes and your competitor's model is just as fast, so selling speed no longer distinguishes anyone. Sell the expensive 80% instead: the edge cases you've already closed, the maintenance you absorb so the customer never sees it, the trust that took years to earn.

The tools that win here will be the ones that make the real cost of doing it yourself visible, then show they've already paid it.
