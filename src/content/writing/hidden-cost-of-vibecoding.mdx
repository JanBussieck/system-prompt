The pitch is seductive: describe what you want, watch it appear. For a demo, vibecoding is unbeatable. For a system someone has to keep running on a Tuesday at 2am, the invoice comes due later, and it rarely lands on the line item you expected.

Marketers of dev tools need to understand this bill in detail, because it is the strongest argument we have left. When a prompt can generate a working surface in thirty seconds, "we save you time" stops being a differentiator. Everyone saves time now. The question is what happens after the time is saved.

## The second 80%

Generation is fantastic at the first 80% of a feature and nearly useless at the second. It gives you the happy path: the form that submits, the chart that renders, the endpoint that returns a 200 when you feed it exactly what it expects. It does not give you the retry logic, the malformed input, the rate limit, the timezone bug, the browser that renders your canvas one pixel off, or the customer whose data breaks an assumption nobody wrote down.

That second 80% is where real engineering lives, and it is exactly the part a generated demo hides. A tool that has already solved it, across thousands of production deployments, is selling something a prompt cannot conjure: the accumulated scar tissue of every edge case its author already hit for you.

## Maintenance is a subscription you pay in attention

Generated code has no owner. When it breaks, there is no changelog, no upgrade path, no one who understands why it was written that way, because it was never written that way on purpose. You inherit a codebase with the institutional memory of a goldfish.

A maintained dependency amortizes that cost across every user. When the platform ships a breaking change, one team absorbs it and everyone else gets a version bump. When you vibecode the same functionality, you volunteer to be that team, forever, for a problem that was never your core business.

## Trust does not generate

The last hidden cost is the one that matters most in regulated or high-stakes work: verification. You can generate a payments flow in an afternoon. You cannot generate the years of audits, the compliance posture, the reputation that lets a buyer's security team wave you through. When the thing being built touches money, identity, or safety, "who stands behind this" is the entire purchase.

## What this means for how you sell

Stop selling speed. Speed is table stakes and your competitor's model is just as fast. Sell the expensive 80%: the edge cases you have already closed, the maintenance you absorb so the customer never sees it, the trust that took years to earn and cannot be prompted into existence.

The winners in this market will be the tools that make the true cost of the do-it-yourself path legible, and then quietly prove they have already paid it.
